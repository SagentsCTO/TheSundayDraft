Beyond Left vs. Right: Builders, Burners, and the Populist Trap

Follow on Spotify → · Follow on Apple Podcasts →

Why do we keep falling for the same political traps?

In this special election episode of The Sunday Draft, we sit down with author and economist Herman to break through the loud, exhausting noise of “Left vs. Right” rhetoric. Instead of leaning on lazy, recycled historical parallels, Herman takes us straight into the deep engine of modern American politics: the underlying psychology of tribalism, the populist mechanics hijacking our conversations, and a much sharper framework for our era—the distinct battle between political “builders” and political “burners.”

Herman Forwood, a podcaster and author of speculative historical fiction. Herman specializing in crafting intricate "what-if" stories—like an alternate 1950s where WWII never happened—to strip away comfortable political echo chambers and force us to look at the modern world through a sharper lens. To dive deeper into his thought-provoking novels, essays, and podcast episodes, visit his official website.

We also zoom in on the high-stakes ground realities across critical battleground states—including Texas, Michigan, Ohio, and Georgia—while questioning a deeper, more structural dilemma: Has our democratic system simply outgrown its 250-year-old foundations?

More than anything, this conversation is an invitation for the exhausted, moderate majority to take off the partisan blinders, reward genuine political civility, and focus cleanly on the real issues impacting our immediate lives—from everyday affordability to the complex futures of technology and foreign conflicts.

This isn’t an episode that tells you what to think. It’s a masterclass from Herman on how to think about the fragile future of our democracy.
