How to Reclaim Your Attention and Deepen Your Learning

Follow on Spotify → · Follow on Apple Podcasts →

Our attention is being strip-mined. Higher education is under unprecedented pressure, and our screens are engineered to keep us scrolling forever. How do we build a meaningful interior life, find deep engagement, and pursue real scholarship under these conditions?

In this episode of The Sunday Draft, we sit down with Dr. Sheldon Greaves, an antiquities scholar (Ph.D. from Berkeley in Ancient Near Eastern Studies) and author of The Guerrilla Scholar’s Handbook.

When his traditional academic path dissolved, Dr. Greaves had to get creative. What emerged is a blueprint for what he calls Guerrilla Scholarship—the practice of doing groundbreaking, rigorous intellectual work entirely outside the walls of a university.

We dive deep into why true breakthroughs happen at the “edge effect” margins where unrelated fields collide, how to break our addiction to constant external stimulation, and why learning must ultimately be driven by joy rather than monetization.

💡 Key Takeaways:

  • The First Step: Start keeping a daily notebook. Write in it to organize your thoughts, protect your focus, and collect ideas that matter to you.
  • The “Edge Effect”: In ecology, the greatest diversity of life happens where two different terrains meet (like land and ocean). In intellectual work, the most interesting discoveries happen where one body of knowledge bumps into a seemingly unrelated field.
  • We Are All Intelligence Analysts Now: Traditional scholars don’t usually look out for deception; intelligence analysts do. In the disinformation age, we all have to build critical thinking tools to spot rhetoric being used against us.
  • The Power of Shared Silence: From Seattle’s “silent reading parties” and vinyl listening bars to Zoom reading groups during COVID, there is a growing movement to gather in a room together just to focus quietly.
  • The Ultimate Prescription: As Merlin tells a downcast young Arthur in T.H. White’s The Once and Future King: “The best thing for being sad is to learn something. That is the only thing that never fails.”
