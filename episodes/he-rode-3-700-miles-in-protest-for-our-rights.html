He Rode 3,700 Miles in Protest for Our Rights

Follow on Spotify → · Follow on Apple Podcasts →

What happens when an engineer with zero prior bike-packing experience decides to cycle 3,732 miles across a supposedly fractured nation?

In this episode, host Raj sits down with Teddy Bloom, a New Mexico-based engineer who spent 87 brutal days riding from the Golden Gate Bridge to the Washington Monument under the banner “Ride for Rights.” Driven by a proactive desire to stand up against the dehumanization of immigrants, Teddy built his solo cross-country protest around four core pillars essential to modern democracy: Free Speech, Justice, Education, and Unity.

Teddy pulls no punches as he breaks down the raw physical reality of riding across America cold—battling a severely strained hamstring through Nevada’s endless desert stretches, routing with a completely wrong bike setup, and handling heavy gear weight over the Sierra Nevadas.

But it’s what Teddy observed on the ground that upends everything mainstream media tells us about a politically polarized country.

What We Cover In This Episode:

  • The Reality of a “Divided” America: How the everyday people Teddy met—from hardcore Trump supporters to strict liberals—offered nothing but warmth, respect, and shelter along the route.
  • ICE and Political Theater: A ground-level account of how immigration enforcement operates noticeably differently in blue vs. red states.
  • The $180 Million “Alligator Alcatraz” Scandal: As an engineer, Teddy exposes how a recent detention facility project seemingly ignored its entire building code book while executing a massive government money grab.
  • The Hidden Visa Pitfalls: A sneak peek into Teddy’s upcoming 8-part investigative documentary series detailing how the legal U.S. visa pipeline (from student visas and H-1B tracks to migrant worker visas) is failing the very people trying to navigate it correctly.
