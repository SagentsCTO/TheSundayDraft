Who's Really Running India's Temples?

Follow on Spotify → · Follow on Apple Podcasts →

A retired Indian Revenue Service officer tried to donate stainless steel vessels to his local temple—only to be warned that the government-appointed Devaswom Board would auction them off within a month and pocket the cash.

In this episode of The Sunday Draft, श्रीraj sits down with Dr. G. Shreekumar Menon—a 1978-era IRS veteran and prominent voice on narcotics policy—to examine how state control has reshaped thousands of Hindu temples in Kerala. From annual priest transfers and temple hundi collections flowing into state treasuries to the Sabarimala gold controversy and synthetic Korean monoglycerides replacing real ghee in sacred rituals, Dr. Menon lays out the mechanics of institutional capture.

The conversation widens into India’s broken medical education pipeline, the collapse of judicial accountability, and what British-style surprise audits reveal about why modern governance keeps failing despite better technology. Finally, Dr. Menon shares a direct message for Gen Z and the Indian diaspora on why he stopped putting money in the hundi—and why keeping life simple matters more than outrage.
